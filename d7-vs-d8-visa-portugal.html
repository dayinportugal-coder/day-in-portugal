D7 vs D8: which Portugal visa in 2026?

The two routes most non-EU applicants compare. The difference is where the money comes from, and how the official income rule is written. This is a comparison, not a substitute for the full D7 and D8 guides.

How to choose, without guessing

Look at the source of the income, not at which name sounds friendlier. If you live on a pension or on passive income, start with the D7. If you invoice clients or have a remote employment contract, start with the D8. Mixing the two in the same application is a common way to be asked for clarifications. The official list of visa types is on the MNE visa portal.

Family members are often included in the same D7 household calculation (50% and 30% shares). For the D8, no official text we have checked states clearly whether family shares sit on top of the €3,680 figure; the calculator therefore shows both readings. For family already in, or still outside, Portugal, see family reunification.

Documents both routes share

A valid passport, criminal-record certificates, proof of accommodation, health or travel insurance, and a completed national-visa form are standard. The exact list is the one your consulate publishes, starting from the MNE document pages. A working tick-list is on the document checklist. Almost everyone also needs a NIF before a lease or a bank account.

Quick answers

What is the income difference between the D7 and the D8 in 2026?

The D7 is 100% of the mainland minimum wage for the first adult (€920 a month in 2026), 50% for each extra adult and 30% for each child, with means secured for at least 12 months. The D8 is an average monthly income of at least four times the minimum wage over the last three months, so €3,680. Sources: Portaria n.º 1563/2007, Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025, and the MNE visa pages.

Can I work on a D7?

The D7 is meant for people living on a pension or regular passive income. If your income comes from remote work for clients or an employer outside Portugal, look at the D8. For anything else, ask the consulate or a qualified professional.

Do EU citizens need either visa?

No. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not apply for a D7 or D8; they register their residence in Portugal instead.
