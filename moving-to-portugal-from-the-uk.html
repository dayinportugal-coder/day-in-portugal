Moving to Portugal from the UK in 2026

Since Brexit, British citizens are third-country nationals in Portugal. A holiday of up to 90 days in the Schengen area is not a move. Living here means a national visa, then an AIMA residence permit.

What changed after Brexit

British citizens no longer register residence as EU nationals. The routes that apply are the same national visas as for other non-EU applicants, listed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. You apply in the UK, at the Portuguese consulate or visa centre that has jurisdiction over where you legally live. After you arrive, AIMA issues the residence permit, usually within the four-month validity of the visa.

D7, D8 and the 2026 figures

If you are retiring or living on passive income, start with the D7. If you work remotely for a UK or other non-Portuguese employer or for clients, start with the D8. The 2026 mainland minimum wage is €920 (Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025). That is the D7 monthly floor for one adult; the D8 floor is €3,680. See the comparison and the calculator.

First practical steps from the UK

  1. Apply for a NIF through the official Portal das Finanças or gov.pt routes.
  2. Budget housing from official rents, not from forum anecdotes: 2026 INE figures.
  3. Build the file from the MNE document list and your consulate’s own checklist. A working tick-list is here.
  4. Plan tax residency under article 16 of the IRS Code, including the UK–Portugal treaty if it applies to you; that is individual advice, not a paragraph on a website.

Quick answers

Do UK citizens need a visa to live in Portugal in 2026?

Yes, to reside. EU freedom of movement no longer applies to British citizens. For a short stay you follow the Schengen rules (in principle up to 90 days in any 180-day period). To live here you apply for a national visa at the Portuguese consulate or visa centre that covers where you live legally in the UK, then for a residence permit at AIMA.

Is the D7 or the D8 more common for Britons?

It depends on the income, not on nationality. Pensions and passive income usually point to the D7 (€920 a month for one adult in 2026, with 12-month means). Remote work usually points to the D8 (€3,680 a month, averaged over three months). Compare them in the D7 vs D8 guide.

Does a UK driving licence or the GHIC replace Portuguese paperwork?

No. A NIF, a reported lease, insurance for the visa file and, after arrival, AIMA and healthcare registration are still required. The UK GHIC helps with necessary care on a temporary stay; it is not a residence-based SNS registration.
