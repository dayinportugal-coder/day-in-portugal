Day in Portugal / Where to sleep in Lisbon

Lisbon · a bed

Sleep where the hill is a choice, not a surprise.

Gifts from Portugal — see the shop

Six neighbourhoods, six different ways to end the evening. Pick the one that matches the station you will actually use.

Baixa

The flat grid between Rossio and the river, rebuilt after 1755. Right if you want Rua Augusta under the window and the Rossio trains to Sintra a short walk away. Rua Augusta itself is a parade at night. Sleep a street back.

Chiado

Up the hill from Baixa, still central, with the bookshops and the evening streets. Steeper than the grid, quieter than Alfama’s tram lane. A fair base for three walking days.

Alfama

The postcard and the hills. Fine for one night if the room is off the 28E route and off the miradouro bars. A bad week if every return is a staircase with a suitcase.

Our pick

Príncipe Real

The garden, a walk down to Chiado, and evenings that are still a neighbourhood. This is where we would sleep for three nights of walking, if the rate is honest.

Avenida and Saldanha

The red line from the airport reaches Saldanha with no change. Metropolitano de Lisboa times that ride at about 18 minutes. Flatter, more of a city than a lane in Alfama. Right if you land late or you are done with cobbles. The photo is the airport station, which is the reason to stay up here.

Belém

The monastery and the tower on day 2 without a commute, and a Cascais-line train back to Cais do Sodré when you want the centre. Quiet at night. Far from Alfama on foot, which is either the appeal or the mistake.

Search those neighbourhoods, not “best hotel Lisbon”. Filter by cancellation, and by the walk to Rossio, Cais do Sodré, or a metro stop you will use twice a day.
