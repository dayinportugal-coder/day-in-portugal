Portugal tax residency in 2026, and what replaced NHR

Visa status and tax residency are not the same thing. This page states the official residence tests in the IRS Code, and the IFICI incentive that has applied to new residents since 1 January 2024. It is not a tax opinion on your case.

The official tests for tax residency

The tests are in article 16.º of the IRS Code (Código do IRS, Decreto-Lei n.º 442-A/88, consolidated). In short, you are resident in Portugal in a year if, in that year:

  • you have stayed in Portugal for more than 183 days, consecutive or not, in any 12-month period that begins or ends in that year; or
  • you stayed for less time, but on any day in that period you have a home here in conditions that suggest you intend to keep and occupy it as your habitual residence.

A day of presence includes any day, full or partial, that includes an overnight stay. Tax residency is assessed for each person in the household. Double-tax treaties can change the outcome; that analysis is individual and is not done on this page.

Becoming tax resident usually means Portuguese IRS on worldwide income, with treaty relief where it applies. Declare and pay through the Portal das Finanças. A NIF is the first practical step; it does not, by itself, make you tax resident.

IFICI, the regime that replaced NHR for new residents

From 1 January 2024, new tax residents cannot enter the old Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) regime. The current statute is the Incentivo Fiscal à Investigação Científica e Inovação (IFICI), article 58.º-A of the Estatuto dos Benefícios Fiscais. The official leaflet is on the Portal das Finanças (PDF). Eligible professions and employers are detailed in Portaria n.º 352/2024/1 (later amended).

In the official description, IFICI allows a special 20% IRS rate on net Portuguese category A (employment) and B (self-employment) income from eligible activities, for 10 consecutive years from the year you register as resident. It is not a blanket 20% rate on pensions, dividends or all foreign income. Typical conditions, from the same official sources:

  • you become tax resident in Portugal in the year you want the regime to start;
  • you were not tax resident in Portugal in any of the previous five years;
  • you carry on an eligible activity, and you continue to earn income from such an activity;
  • you have not used NHR, the “ex-resident” regime in article 12.º-A of the IRS Code, or IFICI before.

The registration request is filed on the Portal das Finanças (Cidadãos → Serviços → Benefícios Fiscais → Inscrição no IFICI), as a rule by 15 January of the year after you become resident. A late registration, the leaflet says, only produces effects from the year it is filed, for the remaining legal period. This is a narrow, formal regime. If it might apply to you, read the official leaflet and, if needed, take professional advice.

Quick answers

When do I become tax resident in Portugal?

Article 16 of the IRS Code treats you as resident in a year if you stay more than 183 days, consecutive or not, in any 12-month period that starts or ends in that year, or if, staying less, you have a home in Portugal that suggests you intend to keep and occupy it as your habitual residence. A day with an overnight stay counts. Source: Código do IRS, art. 16.º.

Does the NHR regime still exist for new arrivals in 2026?

The Non-Habitual Resident regime is closed to new residents who become tax resident from 1 January 2024. The current incentive is IFICI (Incentivo Fiscal à Investigação Científica e Inovação), in article 58.º-A of the Tax Benefits Statute. It is not automatic and it is not a general 20% rate on all foreign income.

What does IFICI actually tax at 20%?

The official Portal das Finanças leaflet describes a special 20% IRS rate on net Portuguese category A and B income from eligible activities, for 10 consecutive years from the year you register as resident. You must not have been resident in Portugal in any of the previous five years, and you must not have used NHR or certain other regimes. Registration is on the Portal das Finanças, as a rule by 15 January of the year after you become resident.
