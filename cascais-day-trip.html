From Lisbon · the Cascais line

Cascais in a day: one train, the bay, and the hole in the cliff.

Gifts from Portugal — see the shop

This is the easy day. The same urban train that stops at Belém keeps going along the river and then the sea. Do not bolt Sintra onto it. The hills and the coast are two mornings, not one.

Cais do Sodré, then stay on the train

Cascais trains leave from Cais do Sodré, on the green metro line. CP’s urban timetable, in the Monday-to-Friday block, times the 05:30 from Cais do Sodré into Cascais at 06:10 — 40 minutes on the train that stops everywhere. Through the middle of the day the sheet shows a departure about every 20 minutes (10:40, 11:00, 11:20, and so on), with a tighter pattern in the weekday peaks and a faster semi-fast that skips some stops. Belém is on the way: the 10:40 from Cais do Sodré is timed at Belém at 10:48. The sheet is CP’s Cascais-line timetable. It has carried notes about replacement buses when the line is being worked on. Check the day, on cp.pt, before you treat 40 minutes as a promise.

Sit on the left going out if you want the water. Tickets are from the machines at Cais do Sodré. This is an urban train: no reserved seat, and no need to book one.

What to do when you get off

The bay. Cascais station is the end of the line, in the town, by the water. The bay in front of the centre is the swim if the weather is kind and you do not need a wave. It is a town beach, not a secret one.

Carcavelos is an earlier stop on the same timetable, before Estoril and Monte Estoril. Get off there if the point of the day is a long beach. Stay on to Cascais if the point is the town.

Boca do Inferno is the cliff and the blowhole west of the centre. It is a walk along the coast from town, not a second train. On a calm day it is a viewing platform. On a rough day it is the reason the name exists. There is no ticket.

Guincho, the open Atlantic beach further west, is not a stop on this train. If that is the swim you want, it is a bus or a car from Cascais, and it is a different hour of the day. Do not promise it to yourself and also promise Boca do Inferno and a late lunch back in Lisbon.

What to skip

  • Sintra the same day. The 434 up to Pena does not start from Cascais station. See the Sintra day on its own morning.
  • A hire car for this line. The train is the point. A car helps if you are going on to Guincho or to a villa off the stations: compare local cars in Lisbon.
  • Belém on the way back only if you still have a slot. The stop is there — day 2 of the Lisbon itinerary — but a cloister ticket you have already missed is just a façade from the platform.

Questions people ask

How long is the train from Lisbon to Cascais?

About 40 minutes on the stopping train. CP’s timetable times 05:30 from Cais do Sodré to 06:10 at Cascais. Faster weekday trains skip some stops. Check cp.pt, including any replacement bus.

Where do you swim?

The bay at Cascais, in town. For a long beach, leave the train at Carcavelos. Guincho is further west and not on the train.

Can you combine Cascais and Sintra?

Not if you want either of them. One is a train along the water. The other is a timed palace door and a bus that starts at 08:50 from Sintra station.
