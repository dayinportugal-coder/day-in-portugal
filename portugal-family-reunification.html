Family reunification in Portugal

Gifts from Portugal — see the shop

By Pedro Carvalho · Last checked against official sources on 13 November 2026

If you already hold a Portuguese residence permit, you may be able to bring close family, or regularise family who are already here, under article 98 of the immigration law. AIMA publishes two official pages, and they are not identical.

Two official pages, two situations

  • Family still outside Portugal — art. 98(1)
  • Family already in Portugal — art. 98(2)

In both cases the request is made by appointment and is presented in person at an AIMA shop, with a specific form. The family member who is being reunited must attend for biometrics. AIMA may send the file to the services for the area where you live. An AIMA FAQ on the services portal notes that, for now, online filing through that portal is only for family already in Portugal.

Documents AIMA lists

The official lists (read the page that matches your case) include, in substance: the residence permit of the person already living in Portugal; a valid passport; authenticated proof of the family relationship (marriage certificate, birth certificate, adoption or guardianship decision, or evidence of a de facto union of more than two years, as applicable); a sworn statement of address, plus a land-registry extract or a landlord’s declaration; proof of means; and a criminal-record certificate from the family member’s country of nationality or of previous long residence, duly authenticated. Family already in Portugal must also show legal entry.

Foreign documents usually need authentication (consular legalisation or a Hague apostille) and, if they are not in Portuguese, a certified translation. Follow AIMA’s wording, not this summary.

Means of subsistence in 2026

AIMA’s means-of-subsistence page uses the percentages in Portaria n.º 1563/2007, applied to the 2026 mainland minimum wage of €920: €920 for the first adult, €460 for each additional adult, €276 for each child. The calculator can show the household arithmetic. If the family is arriving on a D7 or D8 visa of their own, start from those guides instead.

  • AIMA: family reunification, art. 98(2)

Quick answers

What are the two official AIMA routes?

Article 98(1) is for family members who are still outside Portugal. Article 98(2) is for family members who are already in Portugal. AIMA maintains a separate page for each. At the time we checked, the AIMA services portal only accepted online filing for family already in Portugal.

Who counts as family?

AIMA’s document lists refer to a spouse or comparable partnership, minor or dependent children, and, in stated cases, dependent parents or an adult child in education in Portugal, with authenticated proof of the relationship. Take the exact categories from the official page that matches your situation.

How much income is required?

Means of subsistence follow the same official percentages of the minimum wage used more generally: 100% for the first adult, 50% for each additional adult and 30% for each child, based on the 2026 mainland minimum wage of €920. Source: AIMA’s means-of-subsistence page and Portaria n.º 1563/2007. AIMA will apply the rule to your household; do not treat a website calculation as a decision.
