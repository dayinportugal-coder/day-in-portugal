Day in Portugal / Braga and Guimarães day trip

From Porto · the Minho

Braga and Guimarães in a day: Bom Jesus in the morning, the castle after lunch.

An hour north of Porto are two of Portugal’s oldest towns. Guimarães calls itself the birthplace of Portugal; Braga has the country’s oldest cathedral and the great Baroque stairway of Bom Jesus. Together they make one of the best-paced day trips from Porto — if you go by tour or car.

What you see

Braga is a lively university and church town. Above it, Bom Jesus do Monte — a UNESCO World Heritage site — climbs the hill in a zigzag of Baroque stairs, fountains and chapels; a historic funicular saves the climb if you want it. In town, the Sé, the cathedral, is one of the oldest in Portugal, and the old centre around it is easy to walk.

Guimarães, about half an hour away, has a medieval centre that is itself a UNESCO World Heritage site: granite squares, balconies, and the hilltop castle tied to the founding of the kingdom. Next to it is the Palace of the Dukes of Bragança. Look for the wall in town that reads Aqui nasceu Portugal — “Portugal was born here”.

Option 1: a guided day trip (the easy way)

A typical tour leaves central Porto early in the morning, does Bom Jesus and Braga’s centre first, stops for lunch, then Guimarães in the afternoon and is back in Porto in the early evening — about nine hours. Check what is included: some tours include entry to Bom Jesus, the cathedral and the castle; others include lunch or hotel pickup.

Option 2: the train (one town, not both)

Both towns have direct trains from Porto (São Bento and Campanhã) on the suburban network, roughly an hour each way. Train plus walking works well for one town at your own pace. Doing both by train in a day is tight, because there is no direct line between Braga and Guimarães — you need a bus between them. In Braga, Bom Jesus is a bus or taxi ride from the centre, then the funicular or the stairs.

Timetables and tickets: CP, Portuguese railways.

Option 3: a hire car

With two or more people, a car is the most flexible way: motorways from Porto reach both towns in under an hour, Braga to Guimarães is a short drive, and you choose how long to stay at Bom Jesus. Pick the car up the morning you leave, not on arrival — in central Porto it is only hills and paid parking (car or metro?). Motorways in Portugal have tolls; ask the rental desk how they are charged.

Good to know

  • Shoes. Bom Jesus is a lot of stairs if you skip the funicular; Guimarães is cobbles.
  • Public holidays close some monuments; check before you book a holiday date.
  • Other days out — the Douro, Aveiro, the Paiva walkways — are compared on day trips from Porto. For the coast and canals to the south: Aveiro and Costa Nova.

Questions people ask

Can you do Braga and Guimarães in one day from Porto?

Yes. Both are about an hour from Porto and close to each other, so organised day trips combine them: Braga in the morning (Bom Jesus, the cathedral, the old centre), Guimarães in the afternoon (the castle and the medieval centre).

Is it better to take the train or a tour to Braga and Guimarães?

For one town, the train is easy — both have direct trains from Porto. For both in one day, a tour or a hire car saves a lot of time, because there is no quick train between Braga and Guimarães.

What is Bom Jesus do Monte?

A sanctuary on a hill above Braga, reached by a long Baroque zigzag stairway or by a historic funicular. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
