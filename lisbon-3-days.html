Lisbon itinerary · 3 days

3 days in Lisbon: Alfama first, Belém second, the flat grid last.

Gifts from Portugal — see the shop

A walking itinerary if you sleep in the centre. The tram is a ride through streets you can also walk. Sintra, Cascais and Évora are each their own day — not an extra on day 3.

Day 1 — Alfama, one miradouro, and the tram only if a seat is empty

  1. Start at the Sé and walk up to Largo das Portas do Sol and Miradouro de Santa Luzia. The view over the roofs is free.
  2. Keep climbing to Miradouro da Senhora do Monte if you want the whole city in one glance. It is a hill, not a taxi problem, unless your knees say otherwise.
  3. Tram 28E is a Carris tram from Martim Moniz to Campo de Ourique (Prazeres), through Graça, Santa Luzia, the Sé and Chiado. It does not go to Belém. The queue is at the first stop. Walk the same streets, or board further along when a seat is actually empty.

Route and stops: Carris 28E. São Jorge Castle is optional. Most of the postcard is the view from the miradouros outside the walls.

Day 2 — Belém, and only the ticket you will actually use

  1. Train from Cais do Sodré on the Cascais line. On CP’s urban timetable a midday departure at 10:40 from Cais do Sodré is timed into Belém at 10:48. Check the Cascais-line timetable the morning you go.
  2. Or tram 15E, Praça da Figueira to Algés, which stops at Belém (Museu dos Coches) and the Centro Cultural de Belém. Carris 15E.
  3. Jerónimos cloister if the slot is already yours. Belém Tower from the river walk if it is not. Doing both interiors on a hot afternoon is how the day disappears.

Museus e Monumentos de Portugal lists the Jerónimos ticket at €18 and Belém Tower at €15 on its price table, checked 6 November 2026. The Jerónimos page gives the cloister as Tuesday–Sunday 09:30–17:30, last entry 17:00, and shorter hours for the church (Sunday and religious holidays from 14:00). The tower page gives Tuesday–Sunday 09:30–17:30. Both close on Mondays, 1 January, Easter Sunday, 1 May, 13 June and 25 December. Check before you go.

Day 3 — Baixa and Chiado, then one extra, not three

  1. Rossio, Rua Augusta, Praça do Comércio. This is the flat part of the city, which is the point after two days of hills.
  2. Chiado in the late afternoon. The Santa Justa lift is a queue for a view you can also get from the Carmo terrace beside it.
  3. Either LX Factory in Alcântara — shops under the 25 de Abril bridge, not a morning monument — or a sunset back at a miradouro. Not both, and not Sintra on top.

Sintra is its own day, with a timed Pena ticket. The coast is Cascais on the same train line you used for Belém. Inland is Évora, and only if the Intercidades seat is already booked.

Where to sleep for three nights

Chiado or Príncipe Real if you walk. Alfama only off the tram street. Saldanha if the airport metro matters more than cobbles. Belém if day 2 should start downstairs. The neighbourhoods, and which one we would pick: where to sleep in Lisbon.

Arriving

Humberto Delgado (LIS) is on the metro. Metropolitano de Lisboa times the red line from Aeroporto to Saldanha at about 18 minutes and €1.92, with a contactless bank card on the gate, and a taxi at €16–19 and 20–30 minutes without traffic. Checked 6 November 2026 on their airport page. The page says the times are approximate. Check before you go. The detail is in Lisbon airport to the city.

Late flight, children or more bags than hands: a fixed-price Kiwitaxi car from Lisbon airport. Bags for the afternoon you fly home: luggage storage in Lisbon on Radical Storage. Data before you land: a Portugal eSIM on Yesim.
