Cost of living in Portugal 2026: official rents and the wage floor

Crowd-sourced ‘monthly budget’ lists go stale and rarely say where the numbers come from. This page uses two official anchors: the 2026 mainland minimum wage, and the median rent of new contracts published by INE for the second quarter of 2026.

Official rents, second quarter of 2026

On 29 September 2026, Statistics Portugal (INE) reported that the median rent of new contracts in Portugal reached 10.17 €/m² in the second quarter of 2026. That is a year-on-year rise of 10.2%. The number of new contracts (35,407) was 0.4% higher than in the same quarter of 2025. These are medians of contracts communicated to the tax authority, not advertisements.

A worked example, clearly labelled

INE publishes euros per square metre, not a “typical two-bedroom rent”. To make the official median easier to picture, here is a simple multiplication, not an official typical rent and not a promise of what you will pay:

  • 70 m² at the national median of 10.17 €/m² ≈ €712 a month;
  • 70 m² at the Porto metro median of 10.83 €/m² ≈ €758 a month;
  • 70 m² at the Lisbon municipal median of 17.79 €/m² ≈ €1,245 a month.

Actual contracts vary by size, condition, furniture and neighbourhood. Asking prices on portals are often higher than signed medians.

The 2026 wage floor, and why it matters for a visa

The mainland minimum wage for 2026 is €920 a month (Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025). That figure is also the D7 monthly floor for one adult. In Lisbon, the worked example above already exceeds that floor for rent alone. The D8 floor is four times the minimum wage, €3,680 a month. Check your household on the calculator and read the D7 and D8 guides.

This page does not invent grocery, utility or transport baskets. Those costs exist, they vary, and we would rather omit them than guess. Food and energy prices are tracked in INE’s consumer price statistics if you want a national index rather than a blog budget.

Quick answers

What is the official median rent in Portugal in 2026?

In the second quarter of 2026, INE put the median rent of 35,407 new housing contracts at 10.17 € per square metre per month, 10.2% higher than a year earlier. The figures are signed contract rents reported to the tax authority, not asking prices. Source: INE highlight of 29 September 2026.

Which parts of the country are most expensive to rent?

In Q2 2026 the highest regional medians were Greater Lisbon (15.24 €/m²), Madeira (12.78), the Setúbal Peninsula (11.99), the Algarve (11.56) and the Porto metropolitan area (10.83). Among municipalities with more than 100,000 inhabitants, Lisbon had the highest median, 17.79 €/m².

Is the D7 income floor enough to rent in Lisbon?

Not necessarily. The D7 figure for one adult in 2026 is €920 a month, equal to the mainland minimum wage. A worked example from the official Lisbon median (17.79 €/m² × 70 m²) is about €1,245 a month in rent alone. Always check current INE figures and your own lease.
