AIMA residence permit: what happens after your visa

The national visa gets you into Portugal for a few months. The document you actually live on is the residence permit (título de residência), issued by AIMA. Book it in time, and bring what the official pages ask for.

The visa is not the residence permit

You apply for the D7 or D8 at the consulate. After you arrive, AIMA (Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo) handles the residence permit. The MNE states that a national residence visa is valid for four months and two entries, and that you must apply for the permit within that time (MNE: visa types). General conditions are summarised by AIMA under article 77 of the immigration law.

How appointments are requested

On 2 June 2025 AIMA published a notice on online appointments for residence permits with a consular visa. It directs applicants to the official contact form, to select “Autorização de Residência” and then “Autorização de Residência com Visto Consular (Não CPLP)”, and to enter the passport and visa numbers and attach copies of both. gov.pt also maintains a page on booking an AIMA desk visit, and warns that the AIMA portal is the up-to-date source.

There is no unofficial “fast lane” on this site, and we cannot see waiting times. If a form option has changed since we last checked, trust the live AIMA page. Older gov.pt pages that still mention SEF are out of date; SEF was replaced by AIMA.

Renewals and permanent residence

Renewals are handled through AIMA’s renewals portal, which AIMA signs from its homepage. Permanent residence under article 80 can be requested after at least five years on a temporary permit, with means, housing, basic Portuguese, a criminal-record test and regularised tax and social-security affairs. Fees follow AIMA’s table in force on the day of the procedure. EU citizens use a different certificate process, also described on that page.

Quick answers

How long do I have to apply to AIMA after I arrive?

The national residence visa is typically valid for four months and two entries. Within that time you must apply to AIMA for a residence permit. Source: the MNE page on types of national visa. Do not let the visa expire before you have submitted the request in the way AIMA currently asks.

How do I book an appointment in 2026?

AIMA’s notice on appointments for people who already hold a consular visa points to the official contact form at contactenos.aima.gov.pt. You choose “Autorização de Residência” and then “Autorização de Residência com Visto Consular (Não CPLP)”, and you must give the passport and visa numbers and attach copies. Always follow the wording on the live AIMA page, because the form options change.

When can I apply for permanent residence?

AIMA’s page on article 80 says you can apply if you have held a temporary residence permit for at least five years and you meet the other legal tests (means, housing, basic Portuguese, a clean criminal-record threshold, and tax and social-security compliance). The appointment request does not replace the formal application made in person.
