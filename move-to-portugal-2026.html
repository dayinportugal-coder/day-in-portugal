Moving to Portugal in 2026: a practical checklist

The order most non-EU applicants follow, the official 2026 income figures, and the first costs that actually show up in government statistics. Every figure on this page links to an official source.

The steps, in a sensible order

  1. Confirm that you need a visa, and which one. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens register residence; they do not apply for a D7 or D8. For everyone else, the usual long-stay routes for living on a pension or passive income, or for remote work, are the D7 and the D8. Compare them in the D7 vs D8 guide. Visa types are listed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MNE).
  2. Check the 2026 income rule for your household. The mainland minimum wage in force for 2026 is €920 a month (Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025; DGERT). The D7 is a percentage of that wage (Portaria n.º 1563/2007). The D8 is four times that wage. Run the numbers in the income calculator.
  3. Apply for a NIF (Portuguese tax number), which you will need for a lease, a bank account and most official forms. Official routes are on gov.pt, the Portal das Finanças (English) and our NIF and NISS guide.
  4. Put a housing plan in writing. Consulates ask for proof of accommodation. New-contract rents are published by INE; see cost of living in 2026 and renting as a foreigner.
  5. Gather the consulate checklist and book the appointment in the country where you live legally. Start from the MNE document pages. A printable working list is in the document checklist.
  6. After you arrive, ask AIMA for a residence permit (“título de residência”) within the validity of the visa. The visa itself is typically valid for four months and two entries (MNE: visa types). See AIMA after your visa.
  7. Then the civilian basics: a bank account, healthcare registration, and, if it applies, tax residency.

Who this checklist is for

It is written for people who want to live in Portugal in 2026, not for a short holiday. UK citizens are third-country nationals since Brexit; so are US citizens. Both usually need a national visa. Separate notes: from the UK and from the USA. If you already hold a residence permit and want to bring family, read family reunification.

Quick answers

Do I need a visa to move to Portugal in 2026?

If you are a citizen of the EU, the EEA or Switzerland, you do not need a residence visa: you register your residence locally. Everyone else usually needs a national visa from the Portuguese consulate or visa centre that covers where they live legally, then a residence permit from AIMA after arrival.

What is the first official number I should check?

The 2026 mainland minimum wage is €920 a month (Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025). The D7 uses percentages of that wage; the D8 uses four times that wage (€3,680 a month, averaged over the last three months). Use the free calculator to run your household.

Where do I start the paperwork?

Most people first confirm the visa type, then apply for a NIF, then gather the consulate checklist, then book the consulate appointment. After you arrive, AIMA handles the residence permit. Official starting points are the MNE visa portal, gov.pt, Portal das Finanças and AIMA.
